

Dimension 1: Rarity & Scarcity Posture

Definition & Strategic Scope: This dimension evaluates the brand's management of product availability, distinguishing between "objective rarity" (physical scarcity of ingredients and production) and "abundant rarity" (virtual rarity through limited editions and capsule collections). In luxury growth strategies, AI must calibrate its interactions to maintain feelings of privilege even when physical rarity is abandoned for scale.
Diagnostic Evaluative Questions: 
Does the brand brief rely on actual physical scarcity or "virtual rarity" tactics to fuel consumer desire?.
Is the distribution strategy strictly "selective" (few stores/limited access) or moving toward "high diffusion" which risks brand dilution?.
Does the scenario require the AI to enforce "inaccessibility" signals (e.g., waitlists) to reinforce the brand's elitism?.
Grounding Source References: "Beyond rarity: the paths of luxury desire" (Kapferer and Valette-Florence, 2016); Sections 333 (Selective Distribution) and 351 (Selection Path).
Retrieval Confidence Score & Justification: 
Score: High
Justification: Extensive textual evidence exists detailing the transition from objective to virtual rarity and its role in luxury brand building.

Dimension 2: Scenic Invisibility & Symbolic Infrastructure

Definition & Strategic Scope: This dimension assesses the requirement for AI to integrate "invisibly" as a backstage "symbolic infrastructure" that reinforces narrative and ritual without intruding upon the "human touch". The legitimacy of luxury AI hinges on its "scenic discretion," ensuring that the consumer experience remains a "dramaturgical event" centered on human performance.
Diagnostic Evaluative Questions: 
Is the AI designed as a "backstage operator" to orchestrate atmospheres rather than taking center stage as a visible protagonist?.
Does the implementation avoid "overacted" or conspicuous technology that might undermine the "emotional pact" with the client?.
Does the brief require the AI to function as a "relational infrastructure" that enhances, rather than replaces, the performative gestures of the sales advisor?.
Grounding Source References: "Redefining consumer experience through artificial intelligence in the luxury retail sector" (Cenizo, 2025); Sections 502 (Technological Invisibility) and 528 (Dramaturgical Logic).
Retrieval Confidence Score & Justification: 
Score: High
Justification: The concept of "scenic invisibility" and AI as "symbolic infrastructure" is a primary contribution of the Cenizo (2025) paper.







Dimension 3: Consumption Value Orientation (CVT)
Definition & Strategic Scope: Grounded in Consumption Value Theory (CVT), this dimension identifies whether the brand brief prioritizes "functional value" (quality, performance, technical mastery) or "emotional value" (hedonic pleasure, prestige, status). AI integration often enhances functional perceptions but can diminish emotional brand essence if not calibrated.
Diagnostic Evaluative Questions: 
Does the brand rely more on "brand essence" (emotional/hedonic) or "functional utility" (utilitarian/practical) for its perceived value?.
Is the AI being used to enhance "technical excellence" (e.g., quality verification) or is it expected to create a "deep connection" (emotional)?.
Does the brief account for the "negative consumer response" that occurs when AI is seen to reduce a product's perceived uniqueness or symbolic value?.
Grounding Source References: "AI in luxury consumption: bridging consumer desires and managerial perspectives" (Eastman and Aboulnasr, 2026); Sections 142 (CVT) and 149 (AI-designed luxury effects).
Retrieval Confidence Score & Justification: 
Score: High
Justification: Multiple studies in the corpus explicitly apply CVT to evaluate AI's dual role in functional and hedonic value delivery.

Dimension 4: Motivation Profiling (UNM vs. ELT)
Definition & Strategic Scope: This dimension evaluates the target consumer's motivation, distinguishing between "Unknown to the Masses" (UNM—desire for privacy/subtle luxury) and "Association with the Elite" (ELT—need for peer recognition and cultural capital). AI adaptability must shift based on whether consumers value AI's private utility or its capacity to signal elite status.
Diagnostic Evaluative Questions: 
Is the target consumer driven by "inconspicuous luxury motivations" that favor discreet, expert-only signals?.
Does the brief prioritize "functional value" to satisfy UNM consumers who consume luxury in private?.
Is "emotional resonance" emphasized to strengthen the link to "willingness to buy" for status-driven consumers (ELT)?.
Grounding Source References: "AI in luxury consumption: bridging consumer desires and managerial perspectives" (Eastman and Aboulnasr, 2026); Sections 156 (UNM) and 157 (ELT).
Retrieval Confidence Score & Justification: 
Score: High
Justification: The moderation effect of UNM and ELT motivations on AI willingness to buy is empirically validated within the text.



Dimension 5: Symbolic Orchestration Control
Definition & Strategic Scope: This dimension evaluates the "curatorial role" of the brand in the MAS workflow, ensuring that algorithmic outputs are filtered through brand storytelling rather than mere statistical relevance. In luxury, "the algorithm does not have the final word"; it is a system of "curated pertinence".
Diagnostic Evaluative Questions: 
Is the AI's "personalization" logic filtered through brand storytelling and cultural codes to ensure "narrative coherence"?.
Does the brief define AI as a "symbolic mediator" whose suggestions must be "ritualized" by human actors?.
Does the workflow include "curatorial filtering" to prevent automated suggestions from devolving into banality?.
Grounding Source References: "Redefining consumer experience through artificial intelligence in the luxury retail sector" (Cenizo, 2025); Sections 509 (Curation) and 531 (Algorithmic Curation).
Retrieval Confidence Score & Justification: 
Score: High
Justification: Detailed textual evidence supports the "curator" role and the separation of "relevance" from "pertinence" in luxury AI.
